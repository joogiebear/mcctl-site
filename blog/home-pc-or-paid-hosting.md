# Home PC or paid hosting? How to decide

SpawnLoft runs servers on your own PC, and that is a good fit for some people and a poor one for others. Here is the honest version of the choice.

## What a home PC is good at

- **No monthly bill.** You already own the machine and pay for the electricity it uses.
- **Full control.** Your world is a folder you can copy, open and move. Nothing expires and there is no queue.
- **Hardware you already have.** A modern PC is plenty for a server for friends, and you pick how much memory each server gets.
- **Easy when everyone is in the same place.** On your home network, friends join with your PC's address and nothing touches your router. [Play on your home network](/guide/play-on-your-network) shows how.

## What it is not good at

- **The PC has to be on.** If you want the server up while your computer is off, that is hosting's job.
- **Friends elsewhere need a way in that you set up.** SpawnLoft never opens your router for you. You can forward a port, which makes your home address public, or use a tunnel service, which hides it but depends on the service. [Sharing your server](/guide/sharing) covers both.
- **Your home connection is the limit.** Upload speed decides how many players can be on comfortably.

## What a host is good at

A paid host runs your server on their machines, around the clock, with a public address. That is the right tool for a server that must be reachable all the time, or for a large public community. You trade control and a monthly cost for convenience.

## A simple way to choose

| Your situation | Pick |
| --- | --- |
| A few friends, play in the evenings, PC is on anyway | Your PC |
| You build plugins and want somewhere to test them | Your PC |
| You want to learn without spending anything | Your PC |
| Players across time zones expect it to be up 24/7 | A host |
| A public server for strangers | A host |

You are not locked in either way. A world is a folder, so [bringing one in](/guide/bring-your-world) or exporting one out is routine, and you can start at home and move to a host later if the group outgrows it.
